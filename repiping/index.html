Repiping in Hoffman Estates, IL

Is it time to repipe or repair?

One failed pipe does not mean the whole house needs new plumbing. A single leak in sound pipe can usually be repaired. Repiping becomes worth the cost when the pipe itself is the problem.

Consider it when leaks keep appearing in different places, when the water runs brown or rusty, or when pressure has slowly faded across the house. A plumber looks at what is behind the walls, tests the pressure and tells you honestly whether repairs are enough. The line also answers 24/7 if a pipe fails before the project starts.

Galvanized steel pipes and why they fail

Galvanized steel was a common supply material in older homes, and it corrodes from the inside. Rust builds up on the pipe walls, and the opening narrows. The result is low pressure, rusty water at the tap and leaks at the threaded joints.

Repair is a temporary fix, because the next section of pipe is usually in the same condition. Replacing galvanized lines is one of the most common repiping jobs. A plumber can often tell the material by the dull gray color and the threaded fittings.

Copper pipes: pinholes, pitting and repairs

Copper lasts for decades, but it does not last forever. Pinhole leaks, green corrosion at the joints and repeated repairs suggest that the metal is wearing out. Cold water chemistry and high flow speeds can both play a part.

A few isolated repairs on healthy copper are normal. Several in a short time are not. If you have had copper repaired more than once recently, ask the plumber to look at the whole run. A burst from a freeze is a separate issue, covered under burst pipe repair.

CPVC, PEX and what replaces old pipe

New supply lines are usually copper, PEX or CPVC. Each has trade-offs in cost, durability and installation. The plumber explains which fits your house and why.

Low water pressure and discolored water

Low pressure is one of the main reasons people consider repiping. If it affects only one faucet, the fix is usually local. If it affects the whole house, the supply lines may be restricted by corrosion or scale.

Check whether the pressure drop started suddenly or built up over years. A sudden drop may be a leak, a failing valve or a service line problem, which our water service line repair page explains. A slow decline points to pipe age. The Village's water report lists no lead service lines, but the pipes inside your home are your own and a plumber can identify them.

What repiping involves

Repiping is planned work. The plumber maps the new routes, protects your floors and furnishings, and opens walls only where needed. Water is off for part of the job, so ask about the plan for bathrooms and kitchen use.

Access matters. Pipes in an open basement or crawlspace take less work than pipes behind finished walls. Some homeowners repipe in stages by floor, while others do the whole house in one project. We will talk through the sequence by phone before work begins, so (630) 822-9290 with questions.

Permits, inspections and noise rules in Hoffman Estates

Plumbing work in the Village needs a permit. The Village does not publish a separate repipe handout, so repiping is handled through its permit rules for plumbing alterations. Its fee schedule lists a plumbing permit at $75 plus $10 per fixture, and the handout notes it is not all-inclusive.

The Village's remodel handout asks for a complete written scope, a rough plumbing inspection and a final inspection. Construction hours near homes are 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. on weekdays and 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. on weekends. Your plumber plans the schedule around those windows and pulls the permit.

Under Municode Section 12-3-12, plumbing on the Village public water supply must follow the Illinois Plumbing Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890. That is the standard your new pipe, fittings and connections are inspected against, so the pipe material is chosen with that code in mind.

Repiping and drywall repair

Opening walls is part of most repipes. Patching drywall, painting and replacing trim are not plumbing work, so plan for them separately. Many homeowners combine a repipe with a bathroom or kitchen remodel so walls are opened once.

If you are already planning new fixtures, see our plumbing installation page. Doing both together can reduce disruption.
