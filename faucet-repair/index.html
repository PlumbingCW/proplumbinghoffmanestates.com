Faucet won't shut off or is spraying: what to do first

Look under the sink for two small oval or round handles on the pipes. Turn both clockwise to stop the hot and cold supply to that faucet. If they are stuck or leaking when you turn them, go to the main water shutoff, usually near the water meter in the basement or utility room.

With the water off, open the faucet to let the line drain and mop up any standing water. Keep wet cabinet contents away from the floor. Then Call (630) 822-9290 so we can arrive with the right parts and, if a shutoff is failing, replace it at the same visit.

Quick containment steps

  • Close both under-sink shutoff valves, clockwise.
  • If they will not close, shut off the main water valve.
  • Clear the cabinet and put a bowl or bucket under any drip.
  • Do not force an old valve with a wrench, since it can snap.
  • Photograph any water damage for your records.

Dripping and leaking faucets

A steady drip is almost always a worn cartridge, washer or O-ring inside the faucet body. It looks minor, but a drip can add up on your water bill and stain the sink. The fix depends on the faucet style: compression, ball, disc or cartridge.

We replace the worn part when parts are available and recommend a new faucet when the body is corroded or the maker no longer supplies cartridges. If you know the brand, say so on the phone. Some premium brands need specific parts, and it helps us plan.

Tub and shower faucets

A tub faucet that drips or will not stop running is a separate case. The cartridge or stem sits inside the wall, so the repair takes more care than a sink job. If the handle spins without changing the water, or the diverter will not send water to the shower, tell us when you call.

Water that keeps running from a tub spout should be treated as urgent when you cannot shut it off. Close the bathroom shutoff or the main and (630) 822-9290 for help.

Shutoff valves and supply lines

Old angle stops and braided lines are often the real problem. A valve that weeps at the stem or will not turn all the way is a hazard, because you cannot isolate the sink when it matters. We can replace the stop valve and the supply line together.

Valves also serve other fixtures. A fridge ice-maker line, a washer box or a water softener bypass can each fail and spill. If you are unsure which valve feeds a leak, Call (630) 822-9290 us and we will walk through it.

Faucet and valve work falls under the Illinois Plumbing Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890, and Illinois licenses plumbers through IDPH. Before anyone replaces a stop valve in your home, you can look up their license number on the IDPH verification page, which is the standard way to confirm a plumber is licensed.

Kitchen sink problems

Kitchen sinks combine a faucet, a sprayer, a drain basket and often a disposal, so a leak under the cabinet may come from any of them. We dry the area, run the water and trace the first drip. If the disposal is the source, our garbage disposal repair service covers it.

A slow kitchen drain is a different issue from a faucet. Standing water in the sink usually means a clog in the trap or branch line, which we can clear. Describe what the water does and we will schedule accordingly.

Outdoor faucets and winter freeze-ups

Outdoor hose bibs take a beating in Cook County winters. NWS normals for Chicago O'Hare show a January average low near 18.8 degrees Fahrenheit, which is cold enough to split a pipe behind a hose connection if a hose is left attached. The split often stays hidden until spring, when the bib is turned on and water sprays inside the wall.

Before the first hard freeze, disconnect hoses and close the interior shutoff to each outdoor faucet if you have one. If you find a leak inside the wall after turning one on, close the shutoff and (630) 822-9290. We also install new outdoor faucets and frost-proof models.
