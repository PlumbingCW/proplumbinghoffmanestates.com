Plumber in Parcel A and Parcel B, Hoffman Estates, IL

Parcel A and Parcel B are the original 1950s subdivision areas of Hoffman Estates, east of Roselle Road between Golf and Higgins Roads. If a drain, sewer line or water heater is giving trouble in one of these older neighborhoods, call (630) 822-9290 and describe what you see.

Where Parcel A and Parcel B are

The Village of Hoffman Estates traces itself to 1954, when Sam and Jack Hoffman bought a 160-acre farm east of Roselle Road, between Golf and Higgins Roads. That first subdivision is called Parcel A. The first model home opened in 1955, and residents voted to incorporate in September 1959.

The Village also uses the name Parcel B, and its storm sewer page lists both areas in the south of the Village. It does not publish street boundaries for either one. When you call, give the street address and the plumber will place it.

Older homes, remodels and teardowns

A Village planning document from about 2007 describes Parcel A and Parcel B as original subdivision areas with small houses on large lots. It notes a few teardowns and says the Village would consider rehabilitation or redevelopment. For a homeowner, that means plumbing work here often comes with a remodel, an addition or a rebuild.

Age alone does not tell you what pipe is in the wall. A camera on the drain line and a look at the supply lines tell you far more than the year on the deed. A sewer camera inspection is a sensible first step before you buy, remodel or dig. If supply lines turn out to be worn, the next question is repiping.

Remodeling or rebuilding?

The Village asks for a complete written scope of the plumbing work, a rough plumbing inspection and a final inspection. Its fee handout lists a plumbing permit by fixture count, so a new bathroom costs more to permit than a swapped faucet. Fees change, so confirm with Village Development Services before you plan the budget.

Sewer lines: who owns which pipe

The Village maintains the public sewer mains. The homeowner is responsible for the sewer service line from the house up to and including the connection to the Village main. A clog inside your own line is yours to fix, which is why a camera visit is usually the right first call. Call (630) 822-9290

Repairing a service line needs a Village permit. The Village's sewer repair handout asks for a full-size cleanout within 5 feet of the foundation and an inspection before the pipe is covered. If the camera shows damage, sewer line repair follows. If it shows grease or roots, drain cleaning or hydro jetting usually comes first.

Creek areas, drainage and basements

In 2023 the Village's storm sewer page listed Parcel A and Parcel B among the creek areas it cleans. That is routine maintenance, and the page does not say these neighborhoods flood more than others. It does give the right numbers if water is rising in the street. Call Public Works at 847-490-6800 on weekdays from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., or the Police Department at 847-882-1818 after hours.

Inside the house, Village code bars footing drains from connecting to the sanitary sewer. A sump pump has to discharge to the storm sewer or to a ditch or swale. If a pump runs often or the pit has standing water, see sump pump repair and call (630) 822-9290 before the next storm.

Water heaters and permits

A water heater swap in the Village needs a permit. The Village handout asks for a shut-off valve on the cold line, a relief valve with a metal discharge pipe, and proper venting on gas units. Inspections are requested online by 3 p.m. the day before. See water heater replacement or water heater repair for what each job involves.
