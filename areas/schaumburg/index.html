Heavy rain, sewer backups and the Village's cost-share programs

Schaumburg sits in the Metropolitan Water Reclamation District's Upper Salt Creek planning area, and the Cook County hazard plan lists road flooding in the Village on June 19, 2009, July 21, 2017 and May 30, 2018. It also lists federal flood declarations in 2013 and November 2023. The practical lesson is simple. A sump pump and a clear sewer lateral matter before the storm, not after it.

The Village maintains the sewer system. For a sewer backup, Schaumburg's Engineering and Public Works Department asks residents to call it first at 847-923-6612, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Outside those hours the backup does not wait, so call (630) 822-9290 and a plumber can camera the line to see whether the blockage is in your service line or the main. That footage is useful when you talk to the Village.

Two Village programs help pay for prevention. The Overhead Sewer Cost Share Program reimburses 50 percent of an overhead sewer, up to $5,000, one time per address, for detached single-family homes. The Footing Drain Disconnection Cost Share Program pays 50 percent of disconnecting a footing drain and installing a sump pump, also up to $5,000. Community Development answers questions at 847-923-4420. The Village pages do not say whether either program is open now, so ask before you start.

The footing drain program requires a quote from an Illinois licensed plumber and all permits. Its sump discharge hose can sit no more than 10 feet from the house under Village code. Call us for the work scope and a price by phone, then bring both to Community Development. Related pages cover sump pump repair, sewer line repair and a sewer camera inspection.

Permits and code: what Schaumburg requires of the plumber

The Village lists a $50 fee for a water heater permit on its express permits page, but fees change, so confirm with Permit Services.

Schaumburg has adopted the 2014 Illinois Plumbing Code along with specific chapters of the 2021 International Plumbing Code. The Village does not license plumbing contractors itself. That is a practical check for you too, and the Illinois Department of Public Health lets you verify a license online.

The 2014 Illinois Plumbing Code is the standard your job is measured against, and plumbers are licensed under the Illinois Plumbing License Law (225 ILCS 320). If a plumber cannot show a license for the permit, the work stalls.

Lake Michigan water in Schaumburg homes

Schaumburg gets 100 percent of its water from Lake Michigan through Chicago, and has since 1988. Chicago treats it, and the Northwest Suburban Municipal Joint Action Water Agency pumps it to the Village. The Village's 2025 inspection found no lead service lines, so the pipe from the street to your meter is not the lead concern here. Pipe inside the house, especially older solder joints, is still worth checking during any repipe.

Because Chicago treats the water before it reaches town, a home that wants softer water or a filter for taste is making a choice about its own pipes and fixtures, not fixing a Village problem.
