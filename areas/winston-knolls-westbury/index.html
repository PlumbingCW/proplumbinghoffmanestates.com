North of I-90

Plumber in Winston Knolls and Westbury, Hoffman Estates, IL

Winston Knolls and Westbury lie north of I-90 and were among the first areas the Village annexed in the early 1960s. Many homes there have basements, so sump pumps, floor drains and water heaters are the usual plumbing questions. Call (630) 822-9290 with your street and the problem.

Where Winston Knolls and Westbury are

A 2013 Daily Herald neighborhood profile places Winston Knolls north of Algonquin Road and west of Ela Road. It calls the subdivision the first neighborhood built in north Hoffman Estates. The Village history page lists Winston Knolls and Westbury together among the areas annexed in the early 1960s, and the Cook County hazard plan names both.

The Village does not publish street boundaries for either name. Listing sites place Westbury west of Ela Road and south of Palatine Road, which puts the two close together. Give the street address when you call so the plumber can place the house. The Paul Douglas Forest Preserve lies across Algonquin Road, and the Daily Herald notes Willow Park and the Willow Recreation Center inside Winston Knolls.

Basements, footing drains and sump pumps

The Daily Herald profile says many Winston Knolls homes have basements. A basement is where most water trouble shows up, so it helps to know what the Village code requires. Footing drains and drain tile may not connect to the sanitary sewer. A sump pump has to discharge to the storm sewer, or to a ditch or swale where the Village allows it.

Basement floor drains work differently. They go to an ejector pump that discharges to the sanitary sewer. If water stands in a pit or a pump runs nonstop, call (630) 822-9290 and read about sump pump repair. Persistent seepage through the walls may point to basement waterproofing.

Storm history and what it means for your pump

The Cook County hazard plan lists flooding in Hoffman Estates on October 14, 2017 and several federal disaster declarations for storms and flooding between 1986 and 2010. It says multiple areas of the Village are prone to flooding but does not name streets. That means no one can promise your basement stays dry, and it is worth testing the pump before heavy rain, not after.

Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the pump start and shut off. If it does not run, or it runs and the water does not leave, call (630) 822-9290. A pump that fails during a storm is a harder repair than one found in dry weather.

Homes more than 40 years old

The Daily Herald put the homes at about 40 years old in 2013, and listing sites date Winston Knolls to around 1969 and Westbury to the mid to late 1970s. Those dates are not proof of what is inside your walls. They are a reason to ask about the age of the water heater and the supply lines when you call.

A tank that leaks at the base is finished, and water heater replacement follows. A tank that is only cold may need a repair first. If you heat with gas, Nicor Gas lists a $150 rebate for a qualifying tankless unit installed in 2026, with a 90 day window to apply, so check the current terms before you buy a tankless water heater.

Before any water heater work

The Village requires a permit and an inspection for water heater replacement. Inspections are requested online by 3 p.m. the day before. Ask your plumber who files the permit and when the inspector can come.

They are two names. The Village history page and the county hazard plan list them separately, and listing sites place them close together in north Hoffman Estates. Tell the plumber your street and they will place it.

Village code sends it to the storm sewer, or to a ditch or swale where the Village allows it. It may not go to the sanitary sewer. A plumber can check what your house does now.
