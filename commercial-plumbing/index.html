What to do right now when your business has a plumbing emergency

Find the shutoff valve for the affected fixture, room or line. If you do not know where it is, go to the building's main water valve, which is often near the meter or in a mechanical room. Tenants should call the property manager, but do not wait for an answer before shutting water off.

Keep staff and customers out of wet areas near panels, equipment and cords. Move stock off the floor. For a sewage backup, close the area and keep everyone away. Then Call (630) 822-9290 and tell us the address, the type of building and what the water is doing.

Business emergency steps

  • Close the nearest shutoff valve, or the main if needed.
  • Keep people away from standing water near electrical equipment.
  • Move stock, files and electronics above the water line.
  • Stop using sinks, toilets and floor drains if they are backing up.
  • Take photos for your insurer and your landlord.

Commercial leak detection

Leaks cause more business calls than anything else. A hidden leak behind a wall, above a ceiling or under a slab can run for weeks, raising water bills and damaging finishes. Signs include stained ceiling tiles, a meter that moves when everything is off, damp carpet and a sound of running water in a quiet building.

Our plumbers trace the source with listening and pressure checks, then explain the repair options. See our leak detection page for how we locate hidden water. If you manage several units, tell us which ones share a riser or a wall. (630) 822-9290 with what you have noticed.

Drains, floor drains and lift pumps

Commercial drains carry more load than a home line. Restaurants send grease, retail restrooms handle heavy use and warehouses rely on floor drains that sit dry for months. When one backs up, we clear it and find out why, by camera if needed.

A floor drain that overflows and a lift pump that fails can look the same from the doorway. A lift or ejector pump raises wastewater from a low level to the main sewer line, and when it fails, water backs up. We repair or replace these pumps, and for line clearing we use drain cleaning and hydro jetting where the line calls for it.

Business lift and ejector pumps fall under the same Illinois Plumbing Code standard, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890, as a home. The Village code also separates the two pump types, with sump pumps for storm water and ejector pumps for sanitary sewage, so we keep those systems apart when repairing a lift pump.

Restrooms and fixtures

Toilets, urinals and sinks fail in public restrooms faster than at home. We repair flush valves, fill valves, supply lines and faucets, and we replace fixtures when repair no longer makes sense. For toilet work, see toilet repair for how we approach the fixture itself.

Closing a restroom for the day is a cost to a business, so tell us how many fixtures are down and whether the space has a second restroom. That helps us plan the visit and the parts.

Water heaters and hot water

A commercial building may have a tank, several tanks or a larger system, and a failure can leave kitchens, break rooms and restrooms without hot water. We diagnose whether the problem is the unit, the controls or the supply. Our water heater repair page explains the approach.

When a gas appliance is involved and you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first. Then call us once everyone is out and safe. Do not try to relight anything.

Scheduled work: remodels, tenant improvements and maintenance

Not every visit is an emergency. Tenant improvements, new restrooms, relocated sinks and planned fixture replacements are also in our scope. Remodel work in Hoffman Estates needs a complete written scope for the plumbing permit, and rough plumbing and final inspections are required by the Village.

Emergency repairs are a special case. The Village allows emergency repair work to start, but a permit must be submitted within 24 hours after the work begins. We handle that paperwork for permit-required jobs, so you do not have to track it. Our plumbing installation service covers new fixtures and lines.
