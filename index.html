Emergency first

Emergency plumber in Hoffman Estates: what to do first

A plumbing emergency feels worse because the next ten minutes matter. Water keeps moving, and every minute it runs adds to the damage. Do the steps below, then call (630) 822-9290 so dispatch can walk you through the rest while we line up a plumber.

Do these first

  • Shut off the main water valve where the line enters the house, usually near the water meter. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  • Switch off power to any wet room only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the path of the water.
  • Open a faucet at the lowest point in the house to drain the pipes once the main is off.
  • Take photos of the damage before you clean up, in case you file an insurance claim.

Three situations come before the plumber. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or your gas utility from outside, then call us. If water is reaching outlets, appliances or the panel, stay out of the room and call an electrician or 911. If sewage is backing up and anyone feels sick, keep children and pets away from it and do not run any more water.

Never try to repair a gas line, electrical wiring or the main sewer line yourself. Those jobs need a licensed professional and, in many cases, a Village permit. If your own main valve will not close, the Village of Hoffman Estates handles the curb-side B-box shutoff through Public Works at 847-490-6800. Call (630) 822-9290 as well so a plumber can be on the schedule while you wait.

Plumbing services we dispatch for in Hoffman Estates

Most emergency calls fall into three groups. Each has its own page with signs to look for, what the repair involves and what changes the price.

Drain cleaning is the most common request, from a slow kitchen sink to a tub that will not drain. When a snake only clears a hole through the clog, hydro jetting scours the pipe wall. A sewer line cleaning visit reaches the main line that serves the whole house, and sewer line repair covers cracked, collapsed or root-invaded pipe. A sewer camera inspection shows what is wrong before anyone digs.

Water heater trouble comes next. A tank that leaks or has gone cold needs water heater repair or, when the tank itself has failed, water heater replacement. Planned swaps fall under water heater installation, and homeowners who want endless hot water can look at a tankless water heater. Call (630) 822-9290 if you are not sure which one applies.

Then there are leaks and pipes. Leak detection finds hidden water behind walls and under slabs, which is also the usual cause of a sudden high water bill. A burst pipe is the one that cannot wait. Buried supply lines fall under water service line repair, and old galvanized or failing pipe may call for repiping.

Fixtures and the basement round it out. Dispatch also books toilet repair, faucet and sink repair, garbage disposal repair and plumbing installation. For wet basements there is sump pump repair, basement waterproofing and a water softener and filtration service. Boiler repair and commercial plumbing are on the list too. The full list is on the services page.

Local context

What Hoffman Estates water, sewers and weather mean for your plumbing

Hoffman Estates drinks Lake Michigan water bought from the City of Chicago and moved through the Northwest Suburban Municipal Joint Action Water Agency. The Village reports no lead service lines, based on its own inventory, and offers lead testing through Public Works. That means the pipe from the street to your meter is not the lead concern here. Older pipe inside the house, including solder joints, is still worth a look during any repipe.

Sewer responsibility splits at the property. The Village maintains the sanitary mains, and you own the service line from the house to the connection at the main. When a drain backs up in more than one fixture, a camera run tells you which side the blockage is on. A clog in the line you own is a plumber call. A clog in the main is a Village matter, and the camera footage saves you a second trip.

Basement water has its own rules. Village code bars a footing drain from the sanitary sewer, and a sump pump must discharge to the storm sewer or a ditch or swale. The discharge stays 3 feet or more from the property line. A pump that dumps into a floor drain is a code problem. Cook County's hazard plan records flooding in the Village on October 14, 2017, so a working sump pump matters. Call (630) 822-9290 if yours is old or noisy.

Winter matters just as much. The Village code sets the frost line at 42 inches, which is why buried service lines sit deep, but pipes in garages and exterior walls do not. NWS normals for O'Hare put the January average low near 19 degrees. The Village advises keeping the thermostat at 50 degrees or higher when you are away in winter. A burst pipe in January is usually a pipe on an outside wall that lost its heat.

Water heater swaps carry paperwork. A permit and a final inspection are required, and the Village does not do same-day inspections. Nicor Gas customers can also claim a $150 rebate on a qualifying tankless or condensing storage heater installed in 2026, with the application due within 90 days of install. Ask when you call and we will tell you whether your unit qualifies.

Plumbing in the Village is held to the Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890), which Municode Section 11-1-1 adopts. Gas appliances follow the manufacturer's installation instructions as the standard, and work that departs from them can fail inspection.

Hoffman Estates has grown from a 1954 subdivision called Parcel A into a village of about 50,858 people. Homes in the older sections are now old enough that supply lines, tanks and sewer laterals may be near the end of their life. Ask the plumber to check the pipe material while on site.
