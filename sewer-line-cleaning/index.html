Backup in the house: stop, protect, call

When the main sewer line clogs, every fixture shares the problem. Water leaving one drain pushes up in another, and the lowest drain in the house gives way first. In most homes that is a basement floor drain, a shower or a basement toilet.

Do this now

  • Stop flushing and running water. Each use adds to the backup.
  • Shut off the washing machine and dishwasher if they are running.
  • Lift valuables off the floor and keep pets and children out of the area.
  • Put on gloves and boots before you touch anything that got wet.
  • If you see water near outlets or the panel, leave the room and call 911.

Where the blockage usually forms

The main line runs from the lowest part of the house to the connection with the Village sewer. A clog can form anywhere along that run, but certain spots catch more than their share. Joints invite roots. Low sags hold paper and waste. Bends slow flow. The final stretch near the street connection is long and hard to see.

Where the blockage sits determines how we clear it. A cable measures the distance as it travels, so the footage tells us roughly where the clog is. A clog close to the house suggests something in the building drain. A clog far out suggests roots or damage in the buried run. That guides the next step.

What clogs a sewer line

Sewer lines clog for a few common reasons, and each leaves different clues.

Tree roots. Fine roots enter at joints seeking moisture, grow into a mat and catch paper. This causes slow drains and repeat backups, often during a season of heavy growth.

Grease and soap. These coat the pipe and shrink the opening. They start in the kitchen and settle in the main.

Wipes and paper products. Wipes labeled flushable do not break down like toilet paper. They tangle and form a plug.

Sags and breaks. A low spot holds waste, and a broken joint lets soil in. These are repair issues and not cleaning issues, and we say so when the camera shows them. Our sewer line repair page covers those cases.

How emergency sewer line cleaning works, step by step

The process is simple to describe, and it is worth knowing what to expect.

  1. We find the cleanout, usually in the basement, crawl space or near the foundation outside.
  2. We confirm the backup and look at which fixtures are affected.
  3. A heavy-duty cable machine with a cutting head goes in and works through the blockage.
  4. If grease or scale is the cause, we follow with hydro jetting to scrub the pipe wall.
  5. We run water to check flow, and we camera the line if the cause is unclear.
  6. We explain what we found and what to do to keep the line open.

The line is cleaned from the cleanout out toward the street, so the waste moves along with the flow. If there is no cleanout, we may need to pull a toilet or open another access point. We will tell you before we do. You get the price by phone first. (630) 822-9290

No cleanout? Why it matters

A full-size cleanout is a capped opening in the building drain. It gives a plumber direct access to the main line without removing a toilet. A missing, buried or undersized cleanout makes every cleaning harder and slower.

If your home lacks one, we can discuss adding it during the visit. A new cleanout is a small investment that makes every future cleaning faster. It also gives a camera a clean path into the pipe.

The Village's sewer repair handout sets the standard at a full-size cleanout within 5 feet of the foundation. A line that has been repaired is brought up to that, which is a good reason to add one when a line keeps backing up.

Heavy rain, the Village main and your sewer line

The Cook County hazard mitigation plan notes that multiple areas of Hoffman Estates are prone to flooding, and it records flooding on October 14, 2017. When a backup arrives during or just after a heavy rain, ask whether neighbors have it too. If they do, the cause may be the Village main or storm drainage and not your own line.

The Village maintains the sanitary sewer mains, and the homeowner is responsible for the service line from the house up to and including the connection to the main. For street flooding or a blocked storm inlet, the Village lists Public Works at 847-490-6800 on weekdays from 8 to 4, and the Police line at 847-882-1818 after hours. If only your house is affected, the blockage is almost certainly on your side, and we can clear it.

Maintenance cleaning for lines that keep clogging

Some lines need regular attention. Homes with large trees near the sewer line, a long run to the street or a history of grease buildup may benefit from scheduled cleaning. A planned cleaning is easier than an emergency, and you pick the time.

We recommend a camera visit before setting any schedule. It tells you if the cleaning interval should be months or years, or whether the line needs a repair. We never suggest a schedule just to book repeat visits.
