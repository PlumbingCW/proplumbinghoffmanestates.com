Basement Waterproofing in Hoffman Estates, IL

A wet basement has a handful of possible sources, and the fix depends on which one it is. We start by working out whether the water comes from a pipe, a drain, a sump system or seepage through the wall or floor, and then fix the plumbing side. (630) 822-9290 to talk it through.

Start by finding where the water comes from

Wet basements get blamed on the foundation by default, but several plumbing faults produce the same puddle. A leaking supply pipe, a failing water heater, a weeping washer valve and a backed-up floor drain can all look like seepage. The timing is the clue: water that appears after rain points outside, and water that appears with no rain points to a pipe.

Write down when it shows up, where it first appears and whether it smells. Take photos. When you call, those notes let us decide quickly whether a plumbing visit makes sense. A burst pipe or active leak is urgent, and our line is open 24/7 for those cases.

Information that speeds up a diagnosis

  • Where the water first appears: wall, floor crack, drain or corner.
  • Whether it shows up only after heavy rain or snowmelt.
  • Whether there is a sump pit and whether the pump runs.
  • Whether the water is clear, brown or smells like sewage.
  • Whether the water heater, softener or laundry hookups are nearby.

Water through the floor, walls or window wells

Seepage through a floor crack or along the wall base usually means groundwater pressure outside. The National Weather Service normals for Chicago O'Hare show about 37.86 inches of precipitation a year, and soil stays wet long enough for pressure to build against a basement wall. Spring thaw and long storms are the usual triggers.

Plumbing helps by giving that water somewhere to go. An interior drain channel carries it to a sump pit, and a pump moves it out. Grading, gutters and cracked walls are not plumbing items, and we say so when the evidence points that way. Call (630) 822-9290 if you want a plumber's opinion first.

Sump pits, pumps and drain tile

If your basement already has a sump pit, the pump is the heart of waterproofing. A weak or old pump lets water sit in the pit and rise onto the floor. Our sump pump repair service covers repair, replacement, backup systems and the discharge line.

Footing drain tile, the perforated pipe around the foundation, collects groundwater and sends it to the pit. The Village of Hoffman Estates code (Municode Section 11-3-2) says footing drains and drain tile may not connect to the sanitary sewer. If yours ties into a sanitary line, tell us. That is a problem we want to review before any new work begins.

Floor drains and sewer backups

A floor drain that bubbles or spits water during heavy rain is a different issue from groundwater seepage. If sewage comes up, keep everyone out, avoid electrical items and call us right away, since sewage is a health risk. Our drain cleaning and sewer line repair services handle those cases.

Basement floor drains in Hoffman Estates go to ejector pumps that discharge to the sanitary sewer, under the Village code. That separation matters, because sump water never belongs in that line. If a floor drain and a sump discharge share a connection, we will point it out.

Discharge lines, downspouts and where the water goes

A pump only helps if the water it moves does not come right back. The Village code asks for a discharge extension that ends no closer than 3 feet to the property line and points toward the ditch or swale. Newer installations must send storm water underground to the nearest storm sewer unless the Village Engineer allows another outlet.

Village drainage guidance also calls for an air gap where sump or downspout water enters underground piping. We plan discharge routes with those rules in mind. (630) 822-9290 to ask whether yours is set up correctly.

The standard in Municode Section 11-3-2 is that storm water goes to the storm sewer, a ditch or a swale and footing drains may not connect to the sanitary sewer. A repair that sends drain tile water into the household sewer line is therefore not an option, however convenient it looks.

When flooding may be bigger than one basement

The Cook County hazard mitigation plan lists flooding in Hoffman Estates on October 14, 2017 and says multiple areas are prone to flooding. The Village also runs a Storm Sewer Division that maintains catch basins and inlets, and residents can report flooding or blockages to Public Works. If the street is flooded or an inlet is clogged, that is outside private plumbing.

For your side of the property line, a working pump, a clear discharge and a floor drain that is not connected wrongly are the right goals. Call (630) 822-9290 if you want a plumber to look at the whole picture, and ask about pairing it with leak detection when a pipe in the slab or wall is a suspect.

What to do while water is in the basement

  • Stay out if water is near outlets, the panel or appliances, and call your electric utility or 911 if you are unsure.
  • Turn off the main water valve if a pipe is the source.
  • Do not run a vacuum or pump that plugs into a wet area.
  • Move valuables up and take photos for your records.
  • If sewage is involved, avoid contact and keep children and pets away.
