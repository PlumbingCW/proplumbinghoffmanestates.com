Emergency sewer line repair: what to do first

A sewer backup that returns right after cleaning usually means the pipe itself has failed. The line may be cracked, offset at a joint, collapsed or packed with roots. Until that is repaired, every flush and shower pushes more water toward a pipe that cannot carry it.

Safety steps before we arrive

  • Stop all water use, including toilets, showers, laundry and the dishwasher.
  • Leave sewage-soaked rooms and keep children and pets away from them.
  • Do not touch standing sewage with bare hands, and do not run a wet vacuum in it.
  • If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, stay out and call 911.
  • Do not dig up the yard yourself. Underground utilities run through it.

Signs the sewer line itself is damaged

A clogged drain is one thing. A broken sewer line behaves differently. It keeps coming back, and the signs spread beyond the plumbing.

  • Sewage or gurgling at the lowest drain every time you run water.
  • A clog that returns within days or weeks after cabling.
  • A wet or sunken patch in the lawn along the line to the street.
  • A sewer smell outside near the cleanout or around the foundation.
  • Slow drains across the whole house, not one fixture.
  • Fruit flies or drain flies that keep coming back around basement drains.

Any one of these can have a simple cause. Together they point to a pipe problem worth seeing with your own eyes. If the symptoms are milder and you want to confirm the pipe first, the sewer camera inspection page explains how we do that without digging.

How we find the break before we cut anything

We start with a camera. A flexible cable with a waterproof lens goes in through a cleanout, and we watch the pipe in real time. We note the distance to each defect, so a repair can be planned for the exact spot and not a wide guess.

The video shows whether the trouble is a cracked pipe, a joint that has slipped, a root intrusion, a belly that holds water or a full collapse. A locator at the surface marks the depth and position of the break. With that information you get a plain explanation of what failed, where it is and what the options are. If the line only needs a cleaning, we say so and point you to sewer line cleaning.

Not every damaged sewer line needs the same fix. The right choice depends on where the damage is, what the pipe is made of and what sits above it.

Sewer backup or a failed line: how to tell the difference

A backup is a symptom. A failed line is one cause. The way to tell them apart is the pattern. A backup that clears after cabling and stays clear for months was probably a clog. A backup that comes back within weeks, or that follows heavy rain, may be the pipe.

Heavy rain can also overload a pipe with groundwater that finds its way through cracks, which is another reason the camera matters. A cracked line lets water in as well as out. If the video shows clear, solid pipe, the backup was likely a clog, and a cleaning is the right answer. If it shows cracks, roots or sags, the repair conversation starts here. For a stubborn blockage in an otherwise sound pipe, see hydro jetting.

Sewer ejector pumps and lines below the main

Some homes have a basement bathroom or laundry that sits below the sewer line. Those fixtures drain to an ejector pit and a pump lifts the waste up to the gravity line. When that pump fails, the symptoms look like a sewer problem, though the pipe outside may be fine. We check the pump and its check valve along with the line, because the fix is different.

Village permits and inspection for a sewer repair

In Hoffman Estates, a repair to the sewer service line needs a plumbing permit. The Village permit handout calls for a full-size cleanout within 5 feet of the foundation, Schedule 40 PVC for the building drain near the house and SDR 26 pipe for the building sewer outside, with no shear couplings.

The Village inspects before the pipe is covered and again at the end. Its fee handout lists a $75 permit for a sewer or water service repair, and the Village can confirm current fees at 847-781-2631.

Emergency repair work can start first, with the permit submitted within 24 hours after work begins. That matters when sewage is backing up on a Sunday, because the repair does not have to wait for office hours. The Village code also sets the frost line at 42 inches, so a winter repair means working in ground that may be frozen deep. The required cleanout also gives the next plumber a clean way into the line.

Before any excavation we call for utility locates, because buried gas, electric and water lines may cross the yard.

The homeowner owns the sewer service line from the house up to and including the connection to the Village main. The Village maintains the main itself, so a repair that reaches the street connection may involve Village coordination. Construction hours near homes are limited to weekdays 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. and weekends 8 a.m. to 6 p.m., which we plan around.

The standard in Municode Section 12-2-10 is that a plumber may not lend a name to pull a permit, and the Village defines a licensed plumber as one licensed by the State of Illinois. Ask whose license number is on the permit before digging starts.

(630) 822-9290 is the number to use if you are not sure whether your problem is on your side of the connection.
