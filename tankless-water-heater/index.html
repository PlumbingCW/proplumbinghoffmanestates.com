Why tankless units need a flush

A tankless heater heats water as it passes through a heat exchanger, and the passages inside are narrow. Mineral scale that builds up there restricts flow, lowers the temperature and makes the unit work harder. Flushing and descaling is the most common reason people call us about tankless units.

The process runs a descaling solution through the unit with a pump while the heater is isolated from the house. Done on a regular schedule, it clears scale before it hurts performance. Done late, scale can build up enough that a unit runs hot, overheats or shuts down with an error. Call (630) 822-9290

Isolation valves on the cold and hot lines make flushing much easier. If your unit was installed without them, we can add them, and every flush after that goes faster.

How often to flush

The right interval depends on your water and how much hot water you use. Many manufacturers call for a yearly flush, and some units show a service reminder on the display. Check the manual for your model, since skipping the maintenance it requires can cause trouble with warranty claims.

Hoffman Estates gets Lake Michigan water bought from the City of Chicago. The Village's 2025 Water Quality Report does not print a hardness figure, so we do not assume how fast scale forms in your unit. We check the exchanger and the flush water instead, and tell you what we see. If scale looks heavy, a water softener or filter may be worth a conversation.

Common tankless problems

Most tankless calls fall into a few groups. A lack of hot water, water that swings hot and cold, an error code on the display, a unit that will not ignite, and a unit that shuts off after a short run are all common. Each one has several possible causes.

Reading error codes

Tankless heaters display numeric codes that point to a system, such as ignition, air flow, water flow or temperature. The code is a starting point and not a diagnosis. A venting code, for example, might come from a blocked pipe, a failing fan or a sensor.

Send us the code and the model number and we can say what is likely before we arrive. A clean inlet filter, a flush or a vent check solves a lot of codes. Some require a part, and we explain the cost before we replace anything. (630) 822-9290

Switching from a tank to tankless

Replacing a tank with a tankless unit is a bigger job than swapping one tank for another. Gas models usually need a larger gas line, special venting and a power outlet. Electric models need a lot of amperage, which can mean a panel upgrade. Both need the plumbing set up for the new connections.

Many homeowners who want endless hot water find the change worthwhile, and others find the upfront work does not fit their house. We walk you through both sides, including the tank option in water heater installation. If your current tank has already failed, water heater replacement covers the quick-turnaround path.

A vent that has to move is one of the larger parts of a conversion. We note it up front so the price on the phone matches the job. Call (630) 822-9290

Nicor Gas rebates for tankless in 2026

For Nicor Gas customers, the 2026 Energy Efficiency Program lists $150 for a whole-home tankless water heater with a UEF of 0.95 or higher and a capacity of 75 MBTUH or less. The heater must be installed between January 1 and December 31, 2026.

You apply within 90 days of installation or by January 31, 2027, at apply.nicorgasrebates.com or by calling 877-886-4239. Nicor sets the rules and can change them, so confirm the details before you buy. We can help you check whether a particular model fits those numbers.

Heat pump and hybrid questions

We get the occasional question about hybrid heat pump water heaters, including the service of units made by A. O. Smith. These are tank-style units with a heat pump on top, so they sit between a standard electric tank and a tankless unit. They need room, airflow and a condensate drain.

If you have one that needs service, call with the make and model. We will tell you what we can look at and what may need the manufacturer.

Permits and code in Hoffman Estates

The Village of Hoffman Estates covers water heaters under one permit handout, and the Village fee handout lists $50 for a water heater permit. A final inspection is required, and the Village does not do same-day inspections. Venting, relief piping and shut-off valves are among the items the inspector checks.

Gas work and venting should be done by a licensed and insured plumber. Illinois licenses plumbers through IDPH, and the Village follows the Illinois Plumbing Code. We do not recommend a do-it-yourself tankless install, because gas, venting and electrical errors are the kind that hurt people. (630) 822-9290

Manufacturer installation instructions are the standard for a tankless unit's gas line size, vent material and clearances. The Village handout also asks for manufacturer clearances, so a vent run that departs from them can fail the final inspection.
