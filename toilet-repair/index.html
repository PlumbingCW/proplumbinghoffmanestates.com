What to do right now when a toilet overflows

Turn the supply valve on the wall or floor behind the bowl clockwise until it stops. If the water keeps rising, lift the tank lid and push the rubber flapper down to close the opening. Do not flush again, because every flush adds more water to a bowl that is already full.

Put towels around the base and keep children and pets out of the room. If water has reached an outlet, a light switch or a power strip, leave that circuit alone and switch off the breaker only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Then Call (630) 822-9290 so a plumber can get to you the same day.

Do this while you wait

  • Close the supply valve behind the toilet, turning it clockwise.
  • Stop flushing and stop running other drains nearby.
  • Lift wet rugs and towels off the floor so the subfloor can dry.
  • Take photos of any damage if you plan to contact your insurer.
  • Do not pour drain chemicals into a backed-up bowl.

Toilet repair and installation in Hoffman Estates

Most toilet calls come down to a short list of parts. A worn flapper, a stuck fill valve, a cracked supply line or a failed wax ring explains the bulk of leaks, phantom refills and rocking bowls. The parts are small and common, so many repairs are finished in a single visit.

Installation is the other half of the work. We remove the old toilet, check the flange and the floor around it, set the new bowl on a fresh seal and test the flush before we leave. If you already bought a toilet, tell us the brand when you call so we can plan the visit.

Running toilet and phantom refills

A toilet that hisses, refills on its own or keeps running after the flush is wasting water you pay for. The cause is usually the flapper not sealing or the fill valve not shutting off at the right level. Dropping a few drops of food coloring in the tank and waiting ten minutes will show whether the bowl is slowly leaking.

If both bathrooms run at once, the parts are probably the same age and wearing together. Replacing the flapper and fill valve in every toilet during one visit is often cheaper than coming back twice. (630) 822-9290 and describe the sound, and we will tell you which part is likely at fault.

Leaking at the base or from the tank

Water around the base usually means a failed wax ring or a loose closet bolt. Water dripping from the tank bolts or the supply line points to a worn washer or a cracked connector. These look alike on a wet floor, so we dry everything and watch where the first bead of water forms.

A base leak left alone can rot the subfloor over time. If the floor feels soft, or the bowl rocks when you sit, stop using that toilet. Our plumbers can pull it, check the flange and reset it properly.

Fill valves, stop valves and supply lines

The small stop valve under the tank is the one you will need in a crisis, and old ones often seize or leak when turned. If yours will not close, the whole bathroom may need to be shut off at the main until it is replaced. We replace stop valves and braided supply lines as part of a toilet visit, so the next problem is easier to contain.

Our (630) 822-9290 line can also handle questions on broken flush levers, handles that hang loose, and one-piece toilets with parts that are hard to reach. Tell us the make if you can see it on the tank.

Clogs, backups and when it is a drain problem

A clog in one toilet is usually local and a plunger or closet auger clears it. If several fixtures gurgle, or water shows up in a tub or floor drain when you flush, the blockage is further down the line. That is a drain cleaning job and sometimes a sewer line cleaning job.

Sewage coming up through floor drains is a health risk. Keep everyone away from the water, do not use the plumbing, and Call (630) 822-9290 us right away. If the water is near electrical equipment, stay out of the area.

Bidet seats, smart toilets and specialty fixtures

Toilets with built-in bidet wands or washlet seats have extra water lines and power connections. We can often repair spray problems, lid faults and leaking connections, though some electronic faults need the manufacturer. Say so at the start of the call and we will tell you what is realistic.

Permits and the Village

Replacing a toilet in the same spot rarely needs more than a standard visit. Adding a new bathroom or moving plumbing is different, and the Village of Hoffman Estates requires a plumbing permit for that kind of work. A licensed plumber handles the application, and the Village fee schedule is based on the number of fixtures.

The Illinois Plumbing Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890, applies to new work and to alterations of existing systems. That is the standard behind the Village's permit rule, so before moving a toilet or adding a bathroom, call Code Enforcement at 847-781-2631 to confirm what applies.

If you are planning a remodel, ask about plumbing installation so the rough-in and final inspections line up. For a basic swap, the plumber will tell you whether a permit applies.
