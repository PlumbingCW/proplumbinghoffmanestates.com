Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Hoffman Estates, IL

If the line that brings water from the street to your house has broken, you may see a flooded yard, a wet basement wall or no water at all. Our 24/7 line takes these calls. Close the valve inside the house first, and call us from there.

First steps when the service line fails

A broken water service line behaves differently from a burst pipe inside the house. The water often leaves through the soil, so the first sign may be a sinking patch of yard or a basement that smells damp. Start with these steps.

What to do right now

  • Close the main shutoff valve inside the house, usually near the meter.
  • If the valve will not close, call Hoffman Estates Public Works at 847-490-6800 about the street shutoff, then call us.
  • Keep people and pets away from a washed-out or sinking area of lawn.
  • Stay out of a wet basement if water is near outlets or the panel, and call 911 if you feel unsafe.
  • Take photos of the wet area and the meter reading for your records.

Call (630) 822-9290

Do not dig near the line yourself. Buried gas, electric and communications lines may run close by, and digging without a locate is dangerous. The Village's handout tells contractors to call J.U.L.I.E. at 811 first, and the plumber arranges that for the repair.

No water or very low water pressure at every tap

When the whole house loses pressure at once, the service line is a prime suspect. A partial break lets some water through and leaks the rest, so taps spit, run weak or sputter. A split pipe inside the house is a different job, covered under burst pipe repair. Check the meter. If it spins with everything off, water is leaking before it reaches your fixtures.

If a neighbor has the same problem, the cause may be the Village main and not your line. In that case, call Public Works. If only your home is affected, call (630) 822-9290 and we will find out where the loss begins. Low pressure at a single fixture points to something inside the home, not the buried line.

How a plumber confirms the line is the problem

The plumber begins with a pressure test and the meter. If water escapes between the meter and the house, the buried line is the likely source. Acoustic listening can then locate the break so the excavation stays small.

For a line that leaks slowly, leak detection helps find the exact spot before digging. A camera or locating tool cannot see water pipe the way it sees a drain, so the sound and pressure data guide the dig. That step saves yard damage and labor.

Repair or replace: choosing the fix

The right choice depends on the age of the line, the number of breaks and where the break is. A single clean failure in sound pipe can be spot repaired. A line with several leaks, heavy corrosion or an undersized diameter usually calls for replacement.

Some homeowners also ask about full water main replacement from the meter to the house. The same permit steps apply. If you suspect the interior piping is also aging, repiping can be planned together with the service line.

What the Village requires for water service work

Water service jobs in Hoffman Estates are permitted work. The Village's Water Service handout tells contractors to contact the Engineering Division at 847-252-5800 before starting and to call J.U.L.I.E. at 811. The handout calls for Type K copper for services under 3 inches.

The Illinois Plumbing Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890, applies to new plumbing and to alterations of existing systems. A replacement service line is held to that standard just like a new one, which is why the Village inspects it.

The sewer and water repair handout adds that a replaced water service must be at least 1-1/2 inches and Type K copper. It also requires an inspection before the pipe is covered and a final inspection. The permit fee for a sewer or water service repair is $75 on the Village fee handout.

Because inspections are part of the job, the trench stays open until the Village signs off. Plan your yard and driveway access around that. A licensed plumber pulls the permit and schedules the inspections for you. If a new line comes with new fixtures or a remodel, our plumbing installation page explains that work.

When winter makes a line fail

Cold weather is hard on buried water lines. Village code sets the frost line at 42 inches, which is why frost depth matters for any buried line. NWS normals for O'Hare show a January average low of 18.8 F and 38.4 inches of snow per year.

That climate also affects repairs. Frozen ground slows excavation in midwinter, and a repair may need extra time. If you lose water in a cold snap, shut the main, check the meter and call (630) 822-9290 so a plan can be made quickly.

Lead, copper and what the Village reports

Many callers ask whether their line contains lead. The Village states that it completed an inventory of service line materials and found no lead service lines. The inventory is available at the Public Works Center at 2305 Pembroke Ave, and the Village offers water testing for lead.

Sewer pipes are a separate system, handled on our sewer line repair page. Even so, you should ask the plumber to identify the material before any repair. Your home's own plumbing may differ from the Village's records. A plumber can look at the pipe where it enters the house and tell you what it is.
