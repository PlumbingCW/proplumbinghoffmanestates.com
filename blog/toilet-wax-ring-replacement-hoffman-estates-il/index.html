Toilet Wax Ring Replacement in Hoffman Estates, IL

By the Hoffman Estates Pro Plumbing team · Published August 31, 2026

A wax ring is the seal between your toilet and the drain flange, and replacing it means pulling the toilet, scraping off the old wax and setting the toilet back on a new seal. If you are comfortable lifting a heavy fixture, it is a manageable DIY job. If the floor or flange is damaged, call a plumber.

Call (630) 822-9290

Most homeowners notice a bad wax ring in one of three ways. Water seeps out around the base of the toilet after a flush, the toilet rocks slightly, or a sewer smell hangs around the bathroom. Any of those points to the seal, though a loose bolt or a leaking supply line can look the same.

Not sure which one you have? Call (630) 822-9290 and describe what you see. Below is how to tell, how to do the swap safely, and where the job stops being a weekend project.

How to tell the wax ring has failed

Start by drying everything. Wipe the base, the floor, the supply line and the tank with a paper towel. Flush, then watch where the first drop of water appears.

Condensation fools a lot of people. A cold tank in a humid bathroom drips onto the floor and looks like a leak at the base. Dry it, flush, and see if the water comes back before you start pulling anything apart.

What you need before you start

Gather everything first so the toilet is never sitting off its flange while you run to a store. Plan on 1 to 2 hours if nothing goes wrong. Call (630) 822-9290

Tools and parts

  • A new wax ring sized for your drain, or a wax-free seal made for toilets
  • New closet bolts and washers, since old ones often corrode
  • A putty knife and a bucket
  • Old towels, rubber gloves and a sponge
  • A wrench or pliers for the supply nut
  • A helper, because toilets are heavy and awkward

Match the seal to your setup. A thicker ring suits a flange that sits below the floor, and a standard ring suits a flange level with the floor. If you are unsure, the packaging says what each version is for.

Step by step, the safe way

Shut off the water at the wall valve, flush, and hold the handle down to empty the tank. Sponge the last water out of the tank and bowl. Disconnect the supply line and remove the caps on the closet bolts.

Remove the nuts, then lift the toilet straight up and set it on towels. Scrape the old wax off the flange and the toilet horn. Stuff a rag into the drain opening, since that blocks sewer gas while you work.

Look at the flange before you reinstall anything. It should be solid, level and firmly screwed to the floor. If it is cracked, rusted through or sitting below the floor surface, stop and read the next section.

Set the new seal on the flange or on the toilet horn, whichever the package says. Lower the toilet straight down over the bolts, press it down evenly, and do not twist it. Add washers and nuts, then tighten them in small, alternating turns.

Overtightening cracks porcelain. Snug is enough. Reconnect the supply line, open the valve and flush several times, checking the base for any moisture. Wait a day and check again.

Problems that turn this into a plumber job

The wax ring itself is a small part. The expensive surprises are underneath it, and they change the job.

  • A broken or corroded flange that cannot hold the bolts
  • A flange sitting below the finished floor
  • Soft or stained flooring around the toilet
  • Water stains on the ceiling below the bathroom
  • Slow drains in other fixtures along with the leak

A soft floor means water has been getting in for a while, and the subfloor may need repair. That is not something a new ring fixes. Our toilet repair and installation service covers resetting toilets, flanges and the parts around them.

Check the ring's packaging for the flange height it suits, because that instruction is the standard for a good seal. Resetting a closet also falls under the Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890).

What a new seal will not fix

If the toilet gurgles, drains slowly or backs up after you reset it, the seal was not the problem. A line problem can push water around a toilet base just like a bad ring. Our drain cleaning page explains how clogs show up and what a plumber clears.

The Village of Hoffman Estates states that the homeowner is responsible for the sewer service line from the house up to and including the connection to the Village main. The Village's Sanitary Sewer Division maintains the mains themselves, and the Village page lists 187 miles of them.

In practical terms, a toilet that backs up along with other drains is usually your side of the connection. If you are not sure, Public Works is at 847-490-6800 on weekdays from 8 to 4. A plumber can tell you quickly whether the problem is a seal, a flange or a line.

Wax ring or wax-free seal

Both work. Wax is the traditional choice and simple to replace. Wax-free versions use a rubber or foam gasket and tolerate a toilet being reset more than once.

The seal matters less than the setup. A wobbly toilet on a damaged flange fails with any product. Level the toilet, fix the flange, and tighten evenly before you worry about which seal to buy.
