Water Heater Replacement Permit in Hoffman Estates, IL

By the Hoffman Estates Pro Plumbing team · Published August 14, 2026

Yes, the Village of Hoffman Estates lists a permit for water heater replacement. The Village fee schedule shows $50 for a water heater, and the job needs a final inspection before it counts as finished.

Call (630) 822-9290

A water heater permit in Hoffman Estates is handled by Code Enforcement, part of Development Services at Village Hall, 1900 Hassell Road. The Village publishes a handout called "Water Heater - New, Repair & Replacement" that lists the install conditions an inspector looks for. Its fee handout, effective 11/20/2018, lists $50 for a water heater, softener or similar appliance.

That is what the Village documents say. They are not all-inclusive, and fees and rules change, so call Code Enforcement at 847-781-2631 before you schedule work. Need help sorting out the replacement itself? Call (630) 822-9290 and we will walk through it.

What the Village documents verify

The limits matter here. The facts below come from the Village permit handouts and the Municode code, read in October 2026. Anything not listed is something that could not be confirmed.

The governing standard is the Illinois Plumbing Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890, which the Village adopts in Municode Section 11-1-1. The Village checks a finished heater according to that code and its own handout, so the installer's habit does not count.

Who pulls the permit

Contractors must register on the BS&A Online portal with a contractor account before they can apply. In practice, a plumber you hire often handles the application and the inspection request for you. Ask who is pulling the permit before you sign anything.

Illinois licenses plumbers through the Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH). Hoffman Estates code defines a licensed plumber as one licensed by the State of Illinois, and it bars lending a plumber's name to pull permits. IDPH also says that ads for plumbing services must show the plumbing license number, so you can check a license before work starts. Call (630) 822-9290

The install conditions inspectors check

The Village handout lists specific conditions. They are worth knowing even if you never touch a wrench, because they tell you what a clean install looks like.

Conditions listed in the Village handout

  • A shut-off valve on the cold supply within 5 feet, in the same room.
  • Dielectric unions where dissimilar metals meet.
  • Listed flexible connectors.
  • A relief valve with a full-size metal discharge pipe ending 2 to 6 inches above the floor. Plastic or PVC is prohibited.
  • Screwed flue joints with the draft diverter secured, and the contractor certifies the flue or B-vent.
  • For closets, combustion air openings at the top and bottom, 100 square inches each.
  • For gas heaters in a garage, burners 18 inches above the floor.
  • Manufacturer clearances respected.

Notice how many of these are about safety. The relief pipe protects you from scalding discharge. The combustion air and flue rules protect you from carbon monoxide. That is the real reason an inspector looks at them, not paperwork for its own sake.

Why the inspection matters to you

A final inspection is a second set of eyes on work that sits behind a closet door for years. It checks the flue, the relief line and the clearances after everything is connected. Skipping it means nobody has verified the parts that can hurt your family.

Inspections cannot be same-day. The handout is explicit about that, and the Village asks for requests online by 3:00 PM the day before. So plan the final inspection into your schedule instead of expecting it on the install day.

Emergency replacements and the permit

What if the tank fails on a Sunday and you have no hot water or a flooded floor? The Village page says emergency repair work needs a permit submitted within 24 hours after work begins. That is the rule as published, and it is a good reason to ask your plumber how they handle it.

Do not let a permit question keep you from shutting off the water and the gas or power to a leaking heater. Get safe first. If you smell gas, leave the building and call Nicor Gas or 911 before anything else. If a tank is actively flooding near electrical items, shut off the power only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely, or call for help. Call (630) 822-9290

Permit timing and construction hours

Most improvement permits are valid for one year, and a permit is invalid if substantial work has not started within six months. Review targets are not to exceed 14 days per round, according to the Village page. For a like-for-like swap, ask Code Enforcement how long approval takes today.

Construction hours matter if you schedule a weekend job. Within 1,500 feet of residential property, work is limited to weekdays from 7 AM to 7 PM and weekends from 8 AM to 6 PM. A plumber who knows these hours will not start a loud demolition at dawn on a Saturday.

Gas, power and the water heater itself

The permit conversation is easier when you know what you are replacing. A tank swap, a power-vent unit and a tankless unit each bring different venting and clearance questions. Our water heater replacement page explains how we approach the swap, and water heater installation covers new locations.

If your heater is the failing part but not yet dead, a water heater repair visit may be enough. A technician can tell you whether the tank is leaking, which is a replacement call, or whether a part has simply worn out. We also serve neighbouring towns, and our Schaumburg service area page shows where that coverage begins.

What Hoffman Estates adds to a water heater swap

The Village code adopts the Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890) in Municode Sec. 11-1-1, and Sec. 12-3-12 requires plumbing on the public water supply to follow it. In plain terms, a Hoffman Estates install is held to the state plumbing code and the Village's own conditions, not just the manufacturer's sheet.

The Village also lists 2021 editions of the building and fuel gas codes. That is why the flue, combustion air and clearance conditions above are enforced as written. Ask Code Enforcement at 847-781-2631 for the current handout before you buy a unit.

Questions to ask Code Enforcement

Bring this short list when you call. It turns a vague question into specific answers.

  1. What is the current water heater permit fee, and is it still $50?
  2. Does a like-for-like replacement need the same permit as a new location?
  3. How do I request the final inspection, and what is the cutoff time?
  4. Can a homeowner pull the permit, or must a registered contractor do it?
  5. What is the process if the heater fails on a weekend?

Write the answers down with the date and the name of the person you spoke with. If an inspector later reads the rules differently, you will have something to point to.

When to call a plumber

Honestly, replacing a gas or power-vent water heater is not a good first DIY project. The gas connection, venting, relief piping and combustion air all have to be right, and several of them are inspection items. Even a confident homeowner can miss a flue detail that matters later.

Call a licensed plumber when the tank is leaking from the body, when you smell gas, when the pilot or burner behaves strangely, or when the heater is old and failing.
