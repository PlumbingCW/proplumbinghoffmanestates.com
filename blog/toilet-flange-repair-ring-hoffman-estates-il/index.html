Toilet fixes

Toilet Flange Repair in Hoffman Estates, IL

By the Hoffman Estates Pro Plumbing team · Published September 5, 2026

A toilet flange repair ring fixes a cracked or broken flange when the drain pipe underneath is sound and the subfloor is solid. If the flange is rusted through, sits below the floor, or the wood is soft, the flange or the floor needs real repair instead. A rocking toilet or a leak at the base is the usual sign.

Call (630) 822-9290

What the flange does and why it fails

The flange is the ring at the floor that the toilet bolts to and that the wax ring seals against. It holds the toilet still and keeps sewage and sewer gas from escaping at the base.

Flanges fail in a few ways. Old cast iron rusts, plastic cracks around the bolt slots, and brass bends when a toilet has been tightened too hard. A flange set too low after a new floor goes in will also let the seal leak.

When the flange fails, the toilet starts to move. Movement breaks the wax seal, and water or odor follows. Call (630) 822-9290 if you are not sure which problem you have.

Signs the flange is the problem

The toilet rocks when you sit on it, even after you tighten the bolts. The bolts spin or the slots look torn. There is a sewer smell near the base, or water seeps out around it when you flush. The floor near the toilet is stained, spongy or lifting.

None of these proves the flange is at fault. A bad wax ring, a loose bowl or a leaking supply line can look the same. The flange is checked only after the toilet is lifted off.

What a repair ring can and cannot fix

A repair ring is a stainless steel or heavy plastic plate that screws down over the damaged flange. It gives the closet bolts fresh, strong slots and often restores the right height. It works well on a flange that is cracked at the ears but still firmly attached to a good pipe.

It will not fix a drain pipe that has separated or broken below the flange. It will not hold if the screws only bite into rotted wood. And it cannot raise a flange that sits far below the finished floor unless a spacer ring is used.

If the flange is the wrong type for your pipe, or the pipe is cracked, replacement is the honest answer. Ask for the reason in plain words and call Call (630) 822-9290 if you want a second look.

Safe first steps you can do yourself

You can shut off the toilet's supply valve, flush to empty the tank and bowl, and sponge out the rest. Disconnect the supply line over a towel, remove the nuts at the base and lift the toilet straight up. Wear gloves, and set the toilet on a towel or plastic sheet.

Stuff a rag in the opening to hold back sewer gas, and look at the flange. Check the floor around it with a screwdriver. If the wood feels soft, stop there.

Do not use the toilet again until the flange and seal are back in place. Do not cut into the floor, pipe or wiring near the toilet on your own. If the pipe looks cracked or loose, leave the work to a plumber.

The repair itself

A plumber scrapes off the old wax, cleans the flange, and checks that the pipe is firmly connected and the flange sits at the right level. A repair ring is then screwed through into solid subfloor, new closet bolts go in, and a new wax ring or similar seal is set.

The toilet goes back on, is tightened evenly without cranking down, and is tested with several flushes. Water is watched at the base for a few minutes. If the floor was damaged, that repair comes first.

Hiring a licensed plumber in Hoffman Estates

The Village of Hoffman Estates adopts the Illinois Plumbing Code in its municipal code and defines a licensed plumber as one licensed by the State of Illinois. The Illinois Department of Public Health licenses plumbers and requires plumbing ads to display the license number.

That gives you a simple check before anyone opens a floor. Ask for the plumber's license number and look it up on the IDPH license verification page. A toilet flange sits on your home's drain, so you want the person working there to be properly licensed. Call (630) 822-9290 and ask us for ours.

That license is the state standard for who may do the work, set by the Illinois Plumbing License Law (225 ILCS 320). A handyman without one is not a safe substitute on a drain.

If the floor is damaged

A leak that has run for months can soften the subfloor around the flange. Screws need solid wood to hold, so soft or dark material has to be cut out and replaced first. This adds time but protects the repair.

Check the room below if the toilet is on an upper floor. A water stain on a ceiling near the bathroom suggests the leak has been going on for a while. Photograph it, since it helps when you talk to a plumber or your insurer.

When to call a plumber

Call if the toilet rocks after you tighten it, if the floor is soft or stained, or if a sewer smell stays after a new wax ring. Also call if water leaks into the room below. Keep the toilet shut off in the meantime and keep children and pets out of any standing water.

If sewage backs up in more than one fixture, that is a different problem, and you should see our drain cleaning page. If water is spreading and you cannot stop it, close the main valve and call (630) 822-9290 right away.
