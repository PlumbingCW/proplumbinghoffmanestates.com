Sump Pump Installation Cost in Hoffman Estates, IL

By the Hoffman Estates Pro Plumbing team · Published September 27, 2026

The cost of installing a sump pump depends on whether you have a pit already, the pump type, where the water goes and how much digging is involved. We do not publish a flat price, because the same pump can be a simple swap in one house and a bigger job in the next. Call for a price by phone before work starts.

Any dollar range you find online mixes new pits with simple replacements, and it rarely reflects Village rules on discharge. Better to understand the factors, so the number you hear on the phone makes sense.

Below are the factors that move the price, the Hoffman Estates discharge rules that affect the plan, and what to check first. Want a real number for your basement? Call (630) 822-9290 and we will go through it.

What changes the price

Think of the job in four pieces: the pit, the pump, the discharge and the extras. Each piece can be simple or complicated.

A new pit means breaking concrete, digging, setting a basin and patching the floor. A replacement in an existing pit skips most of that. That single difference explains most of the gap between quotes.

Where the water goes matters most

A sump pump moves water from your basement, and that water must go somewhere legal. In Hoffman Estates this is not a detail. It shapes the length of pipe, the connection and the permit question.

The Village code, Municode Sec. 11-3-2, says sump pumps must connect to the storm sewer or discharge to ditches or swales. Footing drains and drain tile may not connect to the sanitary sewer. A sump pump is for storm water, and an ejector pump is for sanitary sewage.

Why does that matter for your quote? A discharge that has to travel farther underground costs more than one that reaches a swale. Call (630) 822-9290

Hoffman Estates sump pump discharge rules

Municode Sec. 11-3-2 requires the discharge extension to stay at least 3 feet from the property line and to point toward the ditch or swale. Installations on or after January 1, 1990 must discharge storm water underground to the nearest storm sewer unless the Village Engineer allows a wetland, detention basin or ditch.

The Village drainage permit handout adds that connections to underground piping need an air gap per the Village detail, with private pipe at least 4 inches in diameter and buried 18 inches. We did not find a standalone sump pump permit page, so ask Code Enforcement at 847-781-2631 or the Engineering Division at 847-252-5800 whether your job needs one.

According to the drainage handout and Municode 11-3-2, a quote that leaves out the discharge route is incomplete. Ask the installer which of those standards your outlet will meet.

Pump or ejector, and why it affects the bill

Basement floor drains and some bathrooms route to an ejector pump that discharges to the sanitary system. That is different from a sump pump, which handles ground and storm water. The two are not interchangeable.

If someone suggests tying a sump into a floor drain or a sanitary line, ask questions. The Village code says sump and footing water belongs in the storm system. A plumber who knows the rules will explain the plan before quoting it.

Replacement versus new installation

If you already have a pit, the job is usually smaller. Many calls start as "my pump died," and a replacement may be all that is needed. Our sump pump installation and repair page covers both cases.

A new installation answers a different question. Is water coming in through the floor, the wall joint or a window well? If you need to control groundwater, basement waterproofing may be part of the plan, and a plumber can explain where a pump alone is enough.

Why Hoffman Estates basements get asked about this

The Cook County hazard mitigation plan annex for Hoffman Estates says multiple areas of the Village are prone to flooding. It records a flood on October 14, 2017 and lists federal disaster declarations for severe storms and flooding going back to 1986. It also reports 822.57 acres in the 100-year floodplain.

None of that means your house floods. It does mean a working pump and a good discharge route are worth checking. The Village also offers drainage investigations through the Engineering Division at 847-252-5800 and participates in the National Flood Insurance Program.

Before you call for a quote

A few answers help us give you a clear price over the phone. Gather them in advance.

What to have ready

  • Whether you have an existing pit and a working pump
  • The age and type of the current pump, if known
  • Where the discharge pipe runs now
  • Whether the basement is finished
  • Any past water in the basement and where it entered
  • Whether you want battery or backup protection

A safe check you can do today

You can learn a lot about your pump without touching wiring. Look into the pit with a flashlight and make sure nothing blocks the float. Then slowly pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch whether the pump starts, empties it and shuts off.

If it runs but the water seems slow to leave, or the pump keeps cycling, tell us when you call. Those details help us guess whether you need a swap, a new check valve or a discharge fix. Never reach into a pit while the pump is plugged in.

Pump failure during a storm

If the pump is not working and water is rising, protect yourself first. Stay out of a flooded basement if water is near outlets, the panel or appliances. Shut off power only if you can reach the panel safely without standing in water, and call an electrician or emergency help if you cannot.

Move valuables up and away from the water. Then call (630) 822-9290 and we will explain what to do while a plumber heads your way.

When to call a plumber

Call a plumber when you need a new pit, a change to the discharge, a backup system or any connection to a storm sewer. These touch Village rules and can cause damage if wrong.

You might be comfortable swapping a plain pump in an existing pit. Even so, the check valve, the air gap and the discharge direction all matter. We are licensed and insured, and we also serve neighbouring Schaumburg, so tell us your address when you call.
