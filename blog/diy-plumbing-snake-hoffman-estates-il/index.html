DIY plumbing snake use in Hoffman Estates: what works and what to leave alone

By the Hoffman Estates Pro Plumbing team · Published August 14, 2026

A hand-crank plumbing snake can clear a clog in a sink, tub or shower drain if the blockage sits in the first few feet of pipe. Feed the cable in slowly, turn the handle, and pull the clog out rather than shoving it deeper. If several fixtures back up at once or the snake stops hard, quit and call (630) 822-9290.

What a plumbing snake can and cannot clear

A plumbing snake, also called a drain auger, is a coiled steel cable with a hook or corkscrew tip. You push it into the drain and crank it so the tip grabs hair, paper or a soft plug. It works well on short, simple runs.

It does not work well on long, deep or shared lines. A clog that is 30 feet away in the main drain is out of reach for a store-bought drum snake. Roots and collapsed pipe also defeat it.

Before you start: gear and setup

You need rubber gloves, safety glasses, a bucket, old towels and a flashlight. Most people also want a small drum snake of 15 to 25 feet. Longer cables need more skill and can scratch or kink old pipe.

Clear the space under the sink and lay towels down. Never mix drain chemicals with a snake job. Leftover acid or lye can splash on your skin and eyes, and it makes the work much riskier.

If you already poured a chemical cleaner in, stop. Do not snake that drain. Let it sit, then call a plumber and tell them what you used.

How to snake a sink or tub drain

Work in a calm, slow rhythm. Most failed attempts come from rushing and forcing the cable.

Step by step

  • Bail out standing water into the bucket so you can see and work cleanly.
  • Remove the stopper or strainer and clean it. For a sink, check the P-trap under the cabinet first, since many clogs sit right there.
  • Feed the cable into the drain by hand until you feel resistance.
  • Lock the thumbscrew near the opening, then turn the crank clockwise while pushing gently.
  • When the tip catches something, keep turning and slowly pull it back out. Expect a mess.
  • Run hot tap water for a full minute and watch the drain speed.
  • Wipe the cable clean as you retract it, then dry and store it.

If the cable moves freely for 10 feet and the water still will not drain, you may be past what the tool can reach. Pulling back out and trying again rarely helps. At that point a second opinion beats a third attempt. You can call (630) 822-9290 and describe what you felt.

Toilets need a different tool

A regular drum snake can scratch the porcelain. A closet auger has a rubber sleeve and a curved end made for the toilet bend. Push the end into the bowl, crank the handle, and pull straight back.

Never use a snake on a toilet that is flooding over the rim. Turn the little valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop new water. Then wait and deal with the cleanup before you try anything.

Plunging first is often enough. If a closet auger fails, something solid may be stuck. A lost toy or a wipe that never dissolved needs a pro with the right access.

Mistakes that turn a clog into a repair

Forcing the cable through a hard stop is the big one. You may punch through a thin old pipe or bend the cable into a knot. Shoving can also pack the clog tighter.

Running the snake through a garbage disposal opening is another trap. Turn off the disposal at the wall switch and the breaker if you plan to work near it. Do not put your hand in the disposal, ever.

Snaking a drain that connects to a clogged main line spreads the mess. If dirty water comes up in a tub when you run the washing machine, the problem is lower down. A cable in the tub drain will not fix that.

Work on the building drain falls under the Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890), and the state licenses plumbers under 225 ILCS 320. When a cable hits a hard stop, that is the standard reason to stop and call a licensed plumber.

Sewage backing up inside the house

If sewage is coming up in a floor drain, tub or toilet, stay out of the water and keep kids and pets away. Do not run the snake. If water is near outlets, appliances or a panel, stay clear of it and call (630) 822-9290. Open windows if there is a sewer smell, and leave the building if anyone feels ill.

Who owns the line in Hoffman Estates

Who owns the sewer line in Hoffman Estates

Under the Village of Hoffman Estates, the sewer service line from your house up to and including the connection to the Village main is the homeowner's responsibility. The Village's Sanitary Sewer Division maintains the main itself, about 187 miles of it. In practice, if a snake pushed through a cleanout is not enough and the blockage sits in your own service line, the repair is yours.

If the stoppage appears to be in the street main, call Village Public Works at 847-490-6800 (Monday to Friday, 8 to 4).

When a plumber replaces or repairs a sewer line, the Village's permit conditions call for a full-size cleanout within 5 feet of the foundation. That matters to you because a good cleanout is where a longer machine or a camera can get in.

When to call a plumber

Call a plumber when any of these are true:

  • More than one fixture is slow or backing up at the same time.
  • You hear gurgling from a toilet when you run a sink or shower.
  • The drain clears for a day and then slows again.
  • The snake hits a wall of resistance and will not move.
  • You see tree roots, a bulging floor or a damp spot near the lawn line.
  • Water is already in the basement.

These signs point to a bigger line, not a single trap. A camera visit through our sewer camera inspection service can show what is in the pipe before anyone digs. When grease or scale is the issue, a plumber may recommend hydro jetting to wash the pipe walls clean.

Anything in a Schaumburg or Hoffman Estates home that stays blocked for a day should be looked at. If you live just east of us, our Schaumburg plumbing page covers that service area as well. Call (630) 822-9290 any hour if the backup is active.
