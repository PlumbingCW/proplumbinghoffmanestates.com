Tankless water heaters

DIY Tankless Water Heater Guide for Hoffman Estates, IL

By the Hoffman Estates Pro Plumbing team · Published September 12, 2026

Installing a gas tankless water heater yourself is not a safe DIY job in most homes, because the gas line, the venting and the inspection are the parts that can hurt people. What you can safely do is maintenance: clean the inlet screen, flush scale with the right kit, and choose and prepare for the unit.

Call (630) 822-9290

The reason is simple. A tankless heater burns a lot of gas in a short burst and needs a sealed, correctly sized vent to carry exhaust outside. A mistake in either place can mean carbon monoxide in the house.

This guide splits the work into what a careful homeowner can do and what needs a licensed plumber. Questions about your own setup? Call (630) 822-9290 and we will talk it through.

What is safe to do yourself

Homeowners can safely handle the research and the upkeep. The unit's manual is the authority, so keep it where you can find it.

Why gas and venting are not DIY

A tankless unit often needs a larger gas line than the tank heater it replaces. If the pipe is too small, the unit starves and shuts down, or runs poorly. If a fitting leaks, you may not smell it until the problem is serious.

Venting is the other half. High-efficiency units often use plastic vent pipe, and the joints, slope and termination matter. A leaking joint puts exhaust gas into the house.

The manufacturer's installation instructions are the standard for a gas tankless unit, and the Illinois Plumbing Code (77 Ill. Adm. Code 890) governs the piping around it. A hookup that departs from them can be refused at inspection.

If you ever smell gas, leave the building immediately. Call Nicor Gas or 911 from outside, and do not flip switches or use a phone inside. Then call us at (630) 822-9290 once everyone is safe.

Cleaning the inlet screen

Most tankless heaters have a small filter screen where cold water enters. Debris can block it and reduce hot water flow, so many people see weak hot water before they see anything else.

Check your manual for where the screen is and how to reach it. Turn off the water supply to the unit, relieve pressure as the manual describes, and remove the screen. Rinse it, put it back, and open the water slowly. If the manual says to call a pro for this step, believe it. Call (630) 822-9290

Flushing scale from a tankless heater

Scale builds on the heat exchanger over time, and the manufacturer usually recommends periodic descaling. A flush uses a small pump, two hoses and a cleaning solution circulated through the unit via service valves.

This is where many homeowners get into trouble. Units without isolation valves need them added first, and the flush must follow the manual's solution and time. A wrong step can leave acid in your plumbing or damage the exchanger.

If you do flush it yourself, rinse thoroughly and run hot water until it is clear. If the unit shows an error code afterward, do not keep resetting it. Our tankless water heater page explains the service side.

Planning a swap from a tank heater

Think about the whole system before you buy. The questions below decide whether a tankless unit fits your house.

  1. Is the gas line big enough for the unit's input rating?
  2. Where will it vent, and is there a clear route outside?
  3. Is there a nearby drain for condensate, if the unit makes it?
  4. Is there a power outlet within reach?
  5. Do you have enough hot water demand to justify it?

A plumber can answer these on site. Moving from a tank to tankless is a bigger change than a tank-for-tank swap, which is why our water heater installation page treats it as an install, not a simple exchange.

Why the permit matters on a tankless job

A DIY install without a permit leaves you with unverified gas and vent work behind a wall. If something goes wrong, an insurer or a future buyer may ask whether the work was inspected. The permit is how you get a second pair of eyes on it.

The Village fee schedule also lists a plumbing permit of $75 plus $10 per fixture for plumbing work. Whether a tankless conversion falls under the water heater fee or the plumbing fee is something to ask Code Enforcement. We do not want to guess here.

What to watch for after a tankless install

Even a good install needs a short checklist at first.

After the unit is running

  • No gas smell at the unit or in the room
  • No error codes on the display
  • Hot water reaches faucets in a reasonable time
  • No drips at the connections or the condensate line
  • Exhaust vent joints are tight and unobstructed

If you see an error code, check the manual before resetting. Repeated resets without finding the cause can hide a real fault.
